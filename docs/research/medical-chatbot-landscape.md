# Medical chatbot landscape and product implications

Researched: **2026-10-02**, using the user's date in Asia/Jakarta. Scope confirmed during research: **patients and caregivers; medical education and preparation for doctor visits; native iPhone app with a hosted service; Indonesia first; English and Indonesian; papers plus separately labeled clinical guidelines and official drug information**. This is research and planning, not approval to implement diagnostic, prescribing, or triage functionality.

## Findings that affect this project

The strongest product opportunity is a patient-readable answer whose factual claims can be inspected against exact source passages and study results. A bibliography alone is a weak distinction: OpenEvidence advertises citations for every claim; ClinicalKey AI advertises real-time assertion checking; Consensus documents exact supporting quotations, full-text indicators, and a Claims & Evidence Table. These are **vendor-described capabilities**, not proof that every answer is correct. [OpenEvidence developer listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/openevidence/id6612007783), [ClinicalKey AI product documentation](https://www.elsevier.com/products/clinicalkey/clinicalkey-ai), [Consensus feature documentation](https://help.consensus.app/en/articles/17234045-consensus-ai-analysis-citations-and-visuals).

For the confirmed scope, combine understandable evidence and study data with structured questions that help someone prepare for a clinician conversation. The competitors below offer useful patterns across three different markets: clinician reference, research synthesis, and patient care/support. Comparing them as interchangeable medical chatbots would obscure their intended use.

## Method and limits

This is a representative landscape, **not an exhaustive census of every medical chatbot**. Sources are official product/help/privacy pages, developer-authored App Store listings, and original research. Availability means documented availability, not a completed enrollment or paid hands-on test. US App Store listings do not establish access in another country. Features, subscriptions, and privacy terms can change; recheck them before product or procurement decisions.

Product feature claims below are vendor documentation unless explicitly identified as research. **Not established** means the reviewed primary sources did not establish the capability; it does not mean the capability is absent. We did not audit production answers, source licensing agreements, security controls, or numerical accuracy. An iOS app existing does not establish that its AI feature is available to every account.

## What “verified” currently means across these products

**Citation existence, supporting passage, factual entailment, study quality, patient applicability and numerical correctness are separate checks.** A real DOI does not establish that a paper supports a sentence. A supporting passage can still describe weak evidence or a different patient population. This distinction is the report's analytical framework, not a claim that vendors all use the same implementation.

### Showing study data

Consensus's Study Snapshot explicitly includes population, sample size, duration, location, methods, outcomes and results. Its help warns that fields may be unavailable when an abstract lacks the information. That is a useful product behavior to preserve: **missing data should remain missing**. [Study Snapshot help](https://help.consensus.app/en/articles/10065008-using-the-study-snapshot).

Elicit documents source-backed extraction from text, tables and figures with configurable templates, weekly paper-corpus updates and real-time trial updates. Those schedules do not establish that any particular answer includes the newest applicable evidence. [Elicit review workflow](https://elicit.com/solutions/literature-review).

For other products, the reviewed sources establish clinical tables, literature access or cited answers to varying degrees, but not a consistently exposed set of study-level numeric fields with validated extraction. Do not infer such a feature merely from screenshots containing numbers.

## Evaluations: separate vendor reporting from external research

**Vendor-reported evaluation:** Doximity's June 2026 technical article reports a blinded physician audit of **50 interactions**, average **4.6/5**, covering citation fidelity, accuracy, evidence strength and uncertainty. The vendor calls results preliminary. This is not a 92% clinical-accuracy score, an independent replication, or a guarantee about each answer. [Original vendor report](https://technology.doximity.com/articles/doximity-ask).

**Vendor-reported research-tool evaluation:** Elicit's current literature-review page reports **96% data-extraction accuracy** and links to its methodology. Such an aggregate does not prove that every extracted number is correct; accuracy depends on the evaluated tasks and source documents. [Elicit product/evaluation links](https://elicit.com/solutions/literature-review), [Vendor evaluation methodology, June 2026](https://elicit.com/blog/evaluating-elicit-slr).

**External original research, benchmark-specific:** A December 2025 preprint compared OpenEvidence and UpToDate Expert AI with GPT-5, Gemini 3 Pro and Claude Sonnet 4.5 on **1,000 MedQA/HealthBench items** and reported stronger performance from the generalist models. It measured particular versions and tasks, not the current products' complete clinical safety or citation fidelity. [Vishwanath et al., original preprint v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01191v1).

**External original research, different task and updated version:** NOHARM **v4, 2026-07-13** describes **1,100 consultation tasks across 10 specialties**, **12,747 expert annotations** and a randomized study with **101 US-licensed generalist physicians**. It reports clinical RAG tools outperforming generalist models on its harm assessment; severe-harm potential reached **24.6% for some systems**, with omissions accounting for over **80%** of severe errors. These are assessed potential harms, not observed patient harm rates. [Wu et al., original preprint v4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01241v4).

The different findings are compatible with different models, versions and evaluation targets. **Product specialization, citations and knowledge-exam scores do not alone establish suitability for this app.** For a patient education product, measure understandable, supported claims and numerical fidelity alongside omissions, unsupported personalization and escalation behavior. That is a recommendation drawn from the distinction between these studies, not a new measured result.

## Provisional recommendations for this project's confirmed scope

1. **Choose a narrow evidence domain for the first version.** Pick a patient question category and a reviewed evidence corpus before attempting general medical coverage. Indonesia-specific source policy and clinician review capacity remain decisions. English/Indonesian support needs bilingual evaluation; do not infer local clinical applicability or competitor availability from the language choice. Indonesia-specific contextualization and care handoff are opportunities to investigate with local primary sources and patient research, not proven unique features.
2. **Make each clinical assertion inspectable.** Store a stable claim ID, source identity, source category, exact supporting passage/location, access level (full text/abstract), evidence date/version, and verification result. Resolve bibliography metadata outside generation. Expose “supported,” “partially supported,” “conflicting evidence,” and “insufficient evidence” with understandable explanations. Automated support checking remains fallible; avoid promising absolute truth.
3. **Treat numbers as structured evidence.** For relevant studies show population, design, intervention/comparator, participant count, outcome, time horizon, effect estimate, units, uncertainty interval where reported, and important limitations. Bind each number to the original passage/table and denominator. Distinguish reported values from calculated values; retain “not reported” fields. Show absolute effects when the necessary data exists and label derived calculations.
4. **Label source types clearly.** Papers answer research questions; clinical guidelines and official drug information support recommendations, contraindications and approved-use facts. Keep each category visible, with version/date and jurisdiction. A guideline does not become a research paper because it is cited beside one.
5. **Use iPhone disclosure progressively.** Start with a short plain-language answer and questions to bring to a doctor. Tapping a claim opens its evidence card; another tap opens the study details and source. A study table should not crowd the initial screen. Support readable text, screen-reader labels, voice input and accessible charts. These are proposed UX requirements, not competitor performance claims.
6. **Prioritize visit preparation over care automation.** Separate user-reported facts, synced records and research-derived education. Let the person confirm a concise visit summary, symptom timeline, medication list and questions before sharing. A symptom intake can organize discussion without producing a diagnosis, prescribing advice or a clinical urgency score.
7. **Keep clinical-context boundaries explicit.** Official drug information can explain general warnings; a personalized interaction checker, dose calculator, diagnostic conclusion or triage disposition would expand the agreed scope and needs a separate decision and validation plan. Urgent-symptom handling should use clinician-reviewed instructions and verified Indonesia-specific care routes. This landscape has not validated local emergency numbers or referral availability.
8. **Make privacy concrete.** Define retention, deletion/export, consent, model-training exclusions, hosting regions, access logging and third-party handling before receiving real patient data. Keep research retrieval queries free of identifying information where possible. Apple Health should be an optional later integration, based on a demonstrated need; competitors already provide personal-data connections.
9. **Evaluate the whole answer pipeline.** Track claim coverage, passage entailment, citation identity, numerical/denominator/unit accuracy, retrieval omissions, evidence age, abstention, and patient comprehension. Include conflicting papers, abstract-only access, missing statistics, subgroup mismatch, outdated/retracted sources and Indonesian paraphrases. Publish versioned results and clinician adjudication methods; a self-generated “verified” badge is insufficient evidence of reliability.

The proposed advantage is therefore **auditable, patient-readable evidence with demonstrated verification performance**, paired with practical doctor-visit preparation. That is a design hypothesis to validate, not an established market gap or an accuracy guarantee.
