# Adult pilot: source readiness

Checked: 2026-10-02. This narrow review compares adult type 2 diabetes and hypertension for an Indonesia-first, English/Indonesian education and doctor-visit preparation app. The user accepted one condition, adults and caregivers of adults, and user-reviewed symptom summaries without diagnosis or urgency scoring. The user has not selected the condition. This is a source-readiness recommendation, not a clinical validation or exhaustive corpus review.

## Patient education and visit preparation

**Diabetes:** NIDDK's [Managing Diabetes](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diabetes/overview/managing-diabetes) offers understandable English explanations of A1C, blood-pressure/cholesterol questions, monitoring records, a downloadable checkup tracker, and preparation for a care-team visit. It explicitly recommends bringing records and preparing questions. Its US care-system examples and target values must be reviewed against Indonesian guidance instead of silently imported.

The Ministry-hosted [AyoSehat diabetes topic](https://ayosehat.kemkes.go.id/topik-penyakit/diabetes-penyakit-ginjal/diabetes-melitus-tipe-2) supplies Indonesian terminology and patient explanations. However, the page includes KlikDokter links: hosting on a government domain alone does not establish original government clinical authorship. Use it as a language/education reference; trace medical claims to the PNPK or original research. A condition-specific Indonesian visit-question template was not verified in this narrow search.

**Hypertension:** The English [MedlinePlus visit-question page](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000226.htm) covers measurement records, equipment, goals, medicines and side effects. This makes a clear workflow reference, but it is licensed encyclopedia material. Indonesian [AyoSehat education by dr. Meutia Gebrina of RSAB Harapan Kita](https://ayosehat.kemkes.go.id/kenali-dan-kendalikan-hipertensi-sebelum-hipertensi-mengendalikan-kita), dated 5 January 2024, discusses measurement and follow-up questions. Its cited consensus is from 2019, so numerical or treatment statements need rechecking against the 2026 PNPK. The [hypertension topic landing page](https://ayosehat.kemkes.go.id/topik-penyakit/pencegahan-infeksi-pada-usia-produktif/hipertensi-tekanan-darah-tinggi) explicitly credits KlikDokter; it also cannot substitute for primary clinical authority.

## Primary studies with visible numbers

**Diabetes example:** The original [Look AHEAD randomized trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23796131/) enrolled 5,145 overweight/obese people with type 2 diabetes at 16 US centers. Intensive lifestyle intervention was compared with diabetes support/education. After median 9.6 years, the cardiovascular composite occurred in 403 versus 418 participants: 1.83 versus 1.92 events per 100 person-years; hazard ratio 0.95, 95% CI 0.83–1.09. The trial stopped for futility. This is a useful verification example: weight-loss improvement must not be rewritten as demonstrated cardiovascular-event reduction. The report links an accessible [PMC manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3791615/); automated access was intermittent during checking. PubMed also flags an erratum, which must be inspected before production extraction. Published summary numbers are available; unrestricted raw participant data and commercial reproduction rights were not established.

**Hypertension example:** The [2021 SPRINT final report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34010531/) randomized 9,361 participants at increased cardiovascular risk, excluding diabetes and previous stroke, to systolic targets below 120 versus 140 mmHg. At median 3.33 years, its cardiovascular composite rates were 1.77% versus 2.40% per year; hazard ratio 0.73, 95% CI 0.63–0.86. Several serious adverse events were more frequent with intensive treatment. Use this final analysis rather than mixing its numbers with the 2015 publication. It links a [PMC manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9907774/), also intermittently blocked during this check. This does not justify assigning an individual patient's target. Raw participant data access was not assessed. PubMed explicitly names Massachusetts Medical Society copyright; reuse permission is not established.

## Access and rights limits

[PMC's own policy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/about/copyright/) distinguishes free reading from licenses permitting reuse and restricts systematic downloading to designated services/subsets. Neither study was cleared here for a commercial full-text corpus, figures, or translation. The [MedlinePlus content policy](https://medlineplus.gov/about/using/usingcontent/) permits reuse of its public-domain health-topic summaries but identifies A.D.A.M. encyclopedia content as copyrighted and requiring direct licensing for ingestion into health IT. Link the visit-question page unless licensed. PNPK/AyoSehat/NIDDK text, illustrations, translated passages and bulk ingestion each still require documented applicable terms; this report does not certify them. Store rights status separately from clinical quality and public accessibility.

## Provisional choice and next boundary

**Provisional recommendation: adult type 2 diabetes**, because the checked NIDDK material directly supports preparation and record review alongside a current Indonesian adult PNPK. Hypertension is also viable; its explicit English question template has a clearer ingestion restriction. This slight source-readiness advantage does not establish market demand, lower risk or superior clinical outcomes.

Proposed first questions: explain A1C, explain the purpose of routine checks, prepare questions about a clinician's existing plan, and organize user-reported concerns for review. Exclude dose changes, personal targets, new diagnosis, pregnancy-specific advice and acute-management instructions from this proposed topic scope. Confirm the condition with the user, then audit coverage and licenses, extract complete study definitions/denominators and limitations, and have medical reviewers check both languages. Cite user-reported summaries to the user's entries; reserve clinical evidence checks for added medical claims.
